## 1. Executive Summary

The photo building (7490 Opportunity Rd, Kearny Mesa, San Diego) rents ~100 micro-office suites at ~$485/mo to used-car dealer LLCs as their DMV-required "established place of business," bundled with license-filing help. You want to replicate it with your real estate skills, automation, and $150k.

**The verdict from the nationwide scan:**

- **Georgia is the best clone of that model in America.** A Georgia used-dealer license with broker operation can run from **an office suite with no display lot**; multi-dealer suite buildings are established practice (4+ operators in metro Atlanta charging $295–495/mo); the license is the most valuable in any surveyed state (retail + wholesale + auction access + dealer plates); and metro Atlanta has the deepest dealer/broker population plus 24–26% office vacancy with class B/C exurban buildings at $90–175/sq ft.
- **The timing wedge:** Georgia's HB 551 (reported effective Jan 1, 2026) raised the minimum dealer office to **~250 sq ft** (exemption for high-volume locations) — obsoleting incumbents' sub-250 sq ft suite inventory and pushing compliant-suite pricing toward $495/mo. A purpose-bought building configured to the new spec enters at exactly the right moment. (Verify the exact rule text with the Used Motor Vehicle Board before design — Gate #1.)
- **Jacksonville is the highest-upside second market:** Florida's 100 sq ft office rule is the friendliest in the country, FLHSMV's 2026 draft rule would *explicitly codify* multi-tenant dealer buildings, Jacksonville has genuinely cheap class C stock ($70–140/sq ft), and **no dealer-suite operator exists there**. Caveat: FL wholesale ("VW") licensees get **no dealer plates**, so the tenant value proposition is auction-access-only.
- **You don't need to be a lawyer** (§7): the filing side is clerical/consulting work — every national incumbent runs it that way. Guardrails required, attorney on referral.
- **You don't need to move to Atlanta** (§6): the business is designed remote-first — automation + a part-time local operator; your Cincinnati base is fine with periodic trips.
- **Investment:** ~$150k = ~$100k down on a ~$400k building + build-out + automation + reserves (§8).
- **Earning power:** stabilized base case **≈ $9–13k/mo net** (building + service arm combined) in months 18–24; the state-agnostic service arm contributes half or more and is the replication engine (§9).

## 2. What This Business Is (and Why Demand Exists)

A dealer license is valuable to people who never intend to run a car lot:
- **Dealer-only auction access** (Manheim, ADESA, Copart, IAAI via AuctionACCESS) — the hero product. Wisconsin's wholesale issuance grew 21/yr → 1,153/yr (+1,194%) purely on out-of-state auction-access demand before its crackdown.
- **Legal flipping at volume** (unlicensed flipping = curbstoning), **sales-tax-free inventory purchases**, **dealer plates** (state-dependent).
- Customers: flippers, exporters, online-only wholesalers, brokers — they want the *license*, not the *lot*.

Every state requires an inspected "established place of business." **The business = renting the cheapest package that passes inspection + handling the paperwork + keeping licensees compliant year after year.**

**Why incumbents are beatable:** verified complaint patterns nationally — hidden fees, withheld deposits, $500 surprise "closing fees," 4+ week filing delays, ghosting after payment, and (Wisconsin) forged application signatures that cost 550+ customers their licenses. Almost nobody publishes prices. Transparent pricing + fast automated filings + genuine compliance is the wedge.

## 4. The Georgia Playbook (Rules You Build Around)

*All from GA SOS Used Motor Vehicle Dealer Board sources; re-verify at Gate #1.*

- **License:** single Used Motor Vehicle Dealer license (State Board of Registration of UMV Dealers, under the Secretary of State). Broker-style operation from an office suite with **no display lot** is explicitly permitted per the Board's FAQ. Tenants can wholesale AND retail — plus dealer plates (DOR Form MV-6, $62 first plate, ~10+ sales/yr condition).
- **Premises requirements per suite:** permanent building; the dealer's separate office with own records; **office entrance not shared with another business** (confirm interior-corridor suite acceptability with the Board — operators' existence suggests it's fine, but get it in writing); **dedicated landline listed in the dealer's name** (tenant procures; you assist setup — never shared lines); compliant sign; local occupation-tax certificate per dealer (Atlanta's ATLBIZ portal and county equivalents handle many businesses at one address routinely — this paperwork is itself a service you sell).
- **HB 551 (eff. 1/1/2026):** minimum office reportedly **250 sq ft** unless 500+ sales/yr at the location; also gives DOR power to suspend temp-tag access on suspicious patterns. **Design implication: build 250+ sq ft suites.** Fewer, bigger, higher-rent units — and instant obsolescence for incumbents' smaller inventory.
- **Applicant costs (your customers'):** ~$170 application + $170 license; biennial renewal ~$150 (expires Mar 31, even years); fingerprint background (GAPS); mandatory pre-license seminar; surety bond **$35,000 now → $50,000 effective 7/1/2026 (SB 293)**; premium ~$300–1,500/yr depending on credit — you earn 10–20% referral commission.
- **Enforcement climate:** SB 293 / HB 551 target temp-tag fraud, not multi-dealer buildings — no premises crackdown found. Georgia's direction of travel raises tenant costs slightly (bond) but *helps* the compliant-landlord positioning.
- **Site pre-approval:** the Board reviews premises with photos at each application. Before purchase, present the building plan to the Board and get written confirmation the configuration qualifies. All three target states (GA/NC/FL) do site pre-approval — this converts regulatory risk into a due-diligence checkbox.

## 7. Legal Structure & the "Not a Lawyer" Question

**No law license needed.** GA/FL/NC have no registration regime for document-prep businesses. The constraint is unauthorized practice of law:
- **Safe:** filling standardized state applications with client-supplied facts; assembling exhibits; courier/filing logistics; scheduling inspections; renewal reminders; publishing general guides; **client signs everything as applicant** (Wisconsin's forged-signature scandal is the cautionary tale).
- **Not safe:** advising on entity structures to avoid legal problems, opining whether a record disqualifies someone, representing clients in license denials/hearings, bespoke contract drafting.
- **Mitigation:** flat-fee Georgia attorney reviews your scripts/site/service agreement annually and takes escalations. Cheap insurance, standard in the industry.

**Entities:** **PropCo LLC** (owns the building, holds the mortgage, leases suites) + **OpCo LLC** (filing service, compliance subscriptions, referral income). Separation isolates liability and makes OpCo independently sellable. Insurance: property + landlord GL + umbrella (PropCo); professional E&O + GL (OpCo). Service agreement: plain-language "not a law firm," published refund terms, document retention policy.
