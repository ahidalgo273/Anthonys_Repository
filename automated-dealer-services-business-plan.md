## 1. Executive Summary

The building in your photo (7490 Opportunity Rd, Kearny Mesa, San Diego — ~100+ micro-suites, each the licensed address of a used-car dealer LLC) is a **dealer-license compliance real estate business**: it rents tiny offices that satisfy the California DMV's "established place of business" requirement, at ~$485/month per suite, often bundled with license-filing help. The Reddit post describes the same model in Missouri.

Three research findings reshape how you should attack this:

1. **The office-suite model cannot be copy-pasted to Cincinnati.** Ohio requires every dealer — wholesale included — to have a 3,500 sq ft display lot plus a 180 sq ft office *on the lot*, and caps dealers at **two per address**. Kentucky requires a 2,000 sq ft lot even for wholesalers. Indiana allows office-only wholesale dealers but only **one per address** with a 120-car/year sales minimum. A 40-suite dealer building is legally impossible in the tri-state.
2. **SBA financing is off the table.** SBA SOP 50 10 8 (effective June 2025) explicitly excludes office-suite/salon-suite rental businesses from 7(a)/504 eligibility. Plan on conventional commercial lending at 20–25% down — your $150k still controls a $400–500k property with reserves left over.
3. **The durable opportunity is a two-sided business:** (a) an Ohio/Northern Kentucky **"dealer park"** — cheap industrial land subdivided into individually fenced, separately addressed display lots with compliant offices, a product with heavy demand (Ohio issues ~6,300 dealer licenses/year) and **zero existing competitors** because nobody has solved the lot requirement at small scale; plus (b) an **AI-automated license filing and compliance service** that is state-agnostic, near-zero capital, launchable in month one, and generates both cash flow and tenant leads.

**Answer to your headline questions:**
- **Do you need to be a lawyer? No.** Preparing and filing standardized license applications with client-supplied facts, where the client signs everything, is clerical/consulting work — the same posture used by every national incumbent. The line you must not cross is individualized legal advice (see §7). Keep an Ohio attorney on referral for escalations.
- **Investment needed:** ~$150k cash deploys as ~$90k down on a ~$350–400k property + ~$35k build-out + ~$25k working capital/software/reserves (see §8).
- **What it can make:** stabilized single-site base case ≈ **$9–14k/month net** combined (real estate + services) within 18–24 months; the service arm alone can reach $3–6k/month net and is the replication engine (see §9).

## 2. What This Business Actually Is (and Why Demand Exists)

A dealer license is valuable to people who never intend to run a car lot:

- **Dealer-only auction access** (Manheim, ADESA, Copart, IAAI via AuctionACCESS) — buy vehicles at wholesale. This is the hero product; Wisconsin's wholesale license issuance grew from 21/year to 1,153/year (+1,194%) almost entirely from out-of-staters buying auction access.
- **Unlimited flipping without curbstoning risk** — unlicensed sale of more than a handful of vehicles/year is illegal in every state.
- **Sales-tax-free inventory purchases** for resale; dealer plates (in most states).
- Customer profile: car flippers, exporters, online-only wholesalers, brokers — people who want the *license*, not the *lot*.

Every state requires an "established place of business" — a real office, sign, phone, posted hours, and (in many states) a display lot — verified by physical inspection. **The business is renting the cheapest possible package that passes that inspection, plus handling the paperwork.** National suite pricing clusters at $250–$550/month; bundles (office + filing + compliance) at $400–$550/month plus $400–$1,650 one-time fees.

### Why incumbents are beatable
Verified complaint patterns against the national players (US Dealer Licensing, dealerlicense.net, etc.): hidden fees, withheld $1,000 deposits, $500 surprise "closing fees," 4+ week filing delays, unresponsiveness after payment, and in Wisconsin's case *forged application signatures* that got 550+ customer licenses revoked. Almost no operator publishes prices. A transparent, fast, automated, genuinely compliant operator has a clear wedge.

### National enforcement climate (your "regulatory shutdown risk")
- **Texas banned subscription dealer offices outright** (43 TAC §215.140, eff. 7/1/2025).
- **Wisconsin** revoked 550+ licenses tied to one 989-dealer building; legislation followed the news coverage.

**The pattern:** states don't kill compliant landlords; they kill conspicuous density + tenant fraud (temp-tag/title fraud brings investigators, then rule changes). Your mitigations: real per-tenant compliance, tenant screening, density discipline, and — most importantly — **a service revenue stream that isn't welded to any one building or state.**

## 4. Three Models Compared

### Model A — Missouri-style multi-dealer office building (the photo, transplanted)
Buy a small office building in Missouri (only tri-state-adjacent state where shared multi-dealer buildings are explicitly legal), subdivide into 96+ sq ft offices with a shared display area, serve national out-of-state demand.
- **Economics:** 20–25 suites × $350–450/mo = $7–11k/mo gross rent + filing fees. Best pure-real-estate ROI.
- **Against:** highest regulatory risk (this is the model Wisconsin dismantled; Missouri is actively tightening — fingerprints 2026, temp tags abolished 2025, bond doubled); remote management ~5 hrs from Cincinnati; W license lost some value (no dealer plates).
- **Verdict:** viable, but as a *later* expansion once you have systems — not the first move for someone who ranked regulatory shutdown as a dealbreaker.

### Model B — Ohio/NKY "dealer park" (the local gap)
Buy ~1–1.5 acres of cheap paved commercial/industrial land (existing small building a plus) in an auto-sales-zoned corridor. Subdivide into 6–12 individually fenced ~3,600 sq ft display sections, each with a compliant 180 sq ft office and its own signage/phone/address treatment. Rent each as a turnkey "license-ready dealership."
- **Economics:** each section rents for $700–1,100/mo (Florida office+lot comps start at $800; Ohio has zero competing supply). 8 sections × $850 = $6,800/mo gross.
- **For:** genuine local demand (6,300 Ohio licenses/yr, no suite operators); fully compliant product that *helps* dealers pass inspection rather than skirting it; uses your PM/landlord/valuation skills directly; hard for a state to object to — it's just small dealership real estate.
- **Against:** the Ohio 2-dealers-per-address cap means the property must be **split into separate parcels/addresses** (county lot split) or you must get BMV written confirmation that separately fenced, separately addressed sections count as distinct "locations." This is Gate #1 — do not buy anything before it's answered. Kentucky variant needs the same verification with KY MVC.

### Model C — AI-automated filing & compliance service (no building required)
A productized service: dealer-license application preparation, inspection scheduling, bond/insurance sourcing (referral commissions of 10–20% of premium), LLC/EIN setup, renewal & compliance calendar subscription. Sold via SEO/content targeting "how to get a dealer license in Ohio/Kentucky/Indiana" and auction-flipper communities.
- **Economics:** $750–$1,500 per application package (incumbents charge $1,350–$5,000, opaquely) + $39–79/mo compliance subscription + bond referral commissions. 8–15 clients/month at maturity ≈ $8–15k/mo gross at software-level margins.
- **For:** launchable in weeks with <$10k; state-agnostic and building-agnostic (your shutdown-risk hedge); location-independent (your replicability requirement); every service client is a warm lead for the dealer park; the AI automation you wanted lives mostly here.
- **Against:** not passive real estate; needs marketing to build lead flow; UPL guardrails must be respected (§7).

### Recommendation: B + C in parallel, A later
Launch **Model C immediately** (cash flow + demand data while you hunt property), acquire the **Model B property** with conventional financing inside 6 months, and revisit **Model A** (Missouri satellite or partnering with existing dealer-friendly landlords rather than buying) in year 2+ once your systems and brand exist. C is what makes the whole thing hit $10k/month and survive a rule change; B is what makes it durable, local, and asset-backed.

## 7. Legal Structure & the "I'm Not a Lawyer" Question

**You do not need to be a lawyer.** None of the four states (OH/KY/IN/MO) has a registration regime for document-preparation businesses. The controlling constraint is Ohio's unauthorized-practice-of-law doctrine:

- **Safe (clerical/consulting):** filling standardized state applications with facts the client supplies; assembling exhibits; couriering filings; scheduling inspections; renewal reminders; selling checklists and general published information; the **client signs everything as applicant** (Wisconsin's forged-signature scandal is exactly what you never do).
- **Not safe (UPL — up to $10,000 civil penalty per offense in Ohio):** advising which entity structure avoids a legal problem; opining on whether a criminal record disqualifies; representing a client in a license denial or hearing; drafting bespoke contracts.
- **Mitigation:** flat-fee relationship with an Ohio-licensed attorney for escalations and an annual review of your intake scripts, website copy, and service agreement. This is standard in the industry and cheap insurance.

**Entity structure (confirm with your attorney/CPA):**
- **PropCo LLC** — owns the real estate; holds the mortgage; leases sections to dealer tenants.
- **OpCo LLC** — the services brand: filing, compliance subscriptions, bond/insurance referrals. Kept separate so a services dispute never threatens the building and vice versa; also cleanly sellable on its own.
- Service agreements state plainly: not a law firm, no legal advice, client is the applicant; document retention policy; refund terms published (incumbents' opaque fees are your marketing).
- Insurance: general liability + professional liability (E&O) for OpCo; property + landlord GL + umbrella for PropCo.
