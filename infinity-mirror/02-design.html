Sheet 1 · Front view

Scale 1:8 approx

What you are looking at

  1. The black frame lip is 0.25 in wide. It is the only metal visible from the front.
  2. The infinity band is 2.50 in wide on all four sides. LEDs on the outer wall reflect back and forth, so the dots look like they recede into a tunnel.
  3. The clear mirror is 14.5 × 22.5 in, large enough to see your head and shoulders at arm's length.
  4. The touch pad sits behind the glass at bottom center: tap to change effect, hold to dim, double-tap for off.
  5. The cord leaves from the back so nothing shows on the wall except the power cord dropping to the outlet.

Trade-off to know

Two-way glass reflects less light than a standard silver mirror, roughly 60–70% versus 90% or more. The center will look slightly darker than a bathroom mirror. Option B avoids this: a silver center mirror plus four separate two-way border strips, with visible seams. Ask the factory to quote both, then compare samples side by side.

Sheet 3 · Electrical schematic (block level)

All mirror-side wiring is 12 V DC

Why it is designed this way

  1. No mains voltage inside the mirror. The adapter is the only part that touches 120 V. Buy one that is already UL or ETL listed so you are not paying to certify a power supply.
  2. The adapter can carry the full load. Worst case, all 144 LEDs on full white draw about 30–36 W. The firmware caps brightness at about 50%, roughly 18 W, so the adapter runs cool and a firmware bug still cannot overload it.
  3. Fuse, polarity and surge protection at the jack protect against a wrong third-party adapter, which is the most common field failure for plug-in LED decor.
  4. IR remote footprint. It costs nothing to add to the board now. If reviews ask for a remote, fit the part and ship a $1 remote without redesigning anything.
