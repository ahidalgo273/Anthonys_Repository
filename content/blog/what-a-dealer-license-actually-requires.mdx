Most people researching a used-car dealer license get the same three answers from every website they find: "it's easy," "call us for pricing," and "we handle everything." None of those are useful, and the last one is not true anywhere in the country.

Here is what the process actually consists of, in the three states we work in.

## The five requirements

Every state we serve — Georgia, Florida, and North Carolina — asks for some version of the same five things:

1. **A registered business entity.** Usually an LLC, with an EIN from the IRS. The name has to match exactly across your application, your bond, and your lease. Mismatched names are one of the most common rejection causes, and it is entirely preventable.
2. **A surety bond.** $50,000 in Georgia and North Carolina, $25,000 in Florida. This is not cash you put up. You pay an annual premium to a bond company — often a few hundred dollars — and the bond company backs the full amount.
3. **A real office.** Roughly 250 square feet in Georgia, 100 square feet with a separate entrance in Florida, 96 square feet in a permanent building in North Carolina. An inspector visits it.
4. **A background check.** Georgia uses GAPS fingerprinting, Florida uses LiveScan, North Carolina runs its own check. You schedule your own appointment; results go to the state, not to us.
5. **Pre-license education, in two of the three.** Georgia requires a seminar, Florida requires a 16-hour course. North Carolina requires nothing for wholesale dealers, which is one of the reasons it is the fastest.

## The one that stops people

It is the office. Every time.

The bond is a phone call. The entity is an afternoon. The background check is an appointment. But a dedicated, signed, phone-listed, inspector-approved office is a lease, a commitment, and a monthly cost — before you have sold a single car.

This is why so many people try to use a home address, and why so many of them get denied. It is also why we rent suites: the requirement is real, and pretending otherwise just wastes your money.

## Sequence matters more than speed

The paperwork is not the bottleneck. Three things are, and all three are outside your control once you start them:

- **Seminar and course seats.** Georgia's seminar and Florida's 16-hour course both book out. Register in week one, not when your file is otherwise ready.
- **Zoning letters.** Florida requires one, and some counties take weeks to produce it. Request it the day you have an address.
- **Inspections.** North Carolina's local inspector pre-approves your location. Reach out early — they will tell you exactly what they want to see, which is cheaper than guessing.

People who start those three items in the first week finish in the fast half of our timeline range. People who save them for last add a month or more, and it feels like the state is slow when it is really the sequence.

## What nobody can promise you

Approval. Not us, not anyone. A service that guarantees you a license is either misunderstanding its own business or hoping you do not ask questions later.

What can be promised is that a preventable paperwork error will not be the reason you get denied — that your entity name matches, that your bond is the right amount, that your photos show what an inspector wants to see, and that you know which step to start first.

That, and knowing the price before you call.
